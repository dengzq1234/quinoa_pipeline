GO Enrichment Analysis — Biological Interpretation

Input: 1,411 uncharacterized quinoa protein-coding genes  ·  Method: biomaRt (Ensembl Plants) → clusterProfiler → org.At.tair.db  ·  p.adjust < 0.05 (BH)

Important caveat: This analysis uses the full background list of 1,411 uncharacterized quinoa genes — not a DEG list. The results describe what biological functions these uncharacterized quinoa genes broadly cover, not what is specifically different between salt-tolerant and non-tolerant accessions. GO enrichment on the DESeq2 DEG list is the next required step.

Biological Process (BP)

The strongest and most gene-rich signal is in photosynthesis and light reactions, with generation of precursor metabolites and energy showing the highest GeneRatio (~0.10), followed by photosynthesis and photosynthesis, light reaction. This indicates that a substantial fraction of "uncharacterized" quinoa genes have Arabidopsis orthologs with well-defined roles in photosynthetic machinery. The likely explanation is that quinoa has diverged sufficiently from Arabidopsis that these genes lost their annotation in quinoa databases, yet they retain the same function.

  • Photosynthesis / light reactions / light harvesting — dominant signal; quinoa's uncharacterized genes are enriched for photosynthetic function
  • Pigment and chlorophyll metabolic process — biosynthesis and turnover of photosynthetic pigments
  • Response to high light intensity — photoprotection mechanisms
  • Porphyrin-containing compound metabolic process — chlorophyll and haem biosynthesis
  • Suberin biosynthetic process — seed coat and root barrier formation; relevant to germination and stress response

Cellular Component (CC)

The CC panel shows the most statistically significant results in the entire plot (deep red dots, p.adjust ~1e-14). Virtually all top terms are chloroplast and thylakoid components, directly confirming the BP signal — these uncharacterized quinoa genes encode proteins that physically reside in the chloroplast membrane system.

  • Chloroplast thylakoid / plastid thylakoid — most significant terms; proteins embedded in the light-harvesting membranes
  • Thylakoid membrane / photosynthetic membrane / chloroplast thylakoid membrane — consistent cluster around chloroplast structure
  • Outer membrane / organelle outer membrane — chloroplast envelope proteins involved in metabolite transport
  • Apoplast — extracellular space between cell wall and membrane; relevant to cell wall remodelling and secretion during germination
  • Secretory vesicle — vesicle-mediated protein secretion; relevant to seed storage protein mobilisation

Molecular Function (MF)

The MF panel reveals two biologically distinct groups of functions among these uncharacterized genes:

  • Hydrolase activity, acting on glycosyl bonds / hydrolyzing O-glycosyl compounds — the top MF terms. These are cell wall-degrading enzymes (glycosyl hydrolases) that break down hemicellulose and other storage carbohydrates during germination. This directly connects to Raquel's work on mannanase enzymes (CqMAN), which are a specific subclass of glycosyl hydrolases.
  • Oxidoreductase activity (multiple terms) / dioxygenase activity — enzymes involved in redox reactions, likely related to chlorophyll/carotenoid biosynthesis, flavonoid metabolism, and oxidative stress responses.
  • Molecular transducer activity / signaling receptor activity — signal perception and transduction; relevant to stress and hormone signalling during germination.
  • Hormone binding — likely ABA receptor activity, directly relevant to the germination stress experiments in the project.
  • Iron ion binding — iron-containing enzymes, consistent with dioxygenase and oxidoreductase functions above.
  • Chlorophyll binding — light-harvesting complex proteins; consistent with the photosynthesis signal throughout.
